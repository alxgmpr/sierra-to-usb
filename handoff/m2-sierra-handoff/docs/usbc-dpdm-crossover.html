Two-jumper D+/D− crossover

Two discrete 0 Ω 0402 jumpers, RJ1 and RJ2 — one carries D+ over the D− trace, the other is its matched partner on the D− leg. Four views: schematic, PCB placement, the bridge close-up, and a side cut so you can see why nothing shorts.

The rule that ties it together 2 symbols in the schematic ⇄ 2 footprints on the board. Both jumpers are physically placed. Only one of them (RJ1) actually bridges the crossing — the other (RJ2) is 0 Ω on a straight trace, there purely to keep the pair matched.

1Schematic

Position is meaningless here — the two 0 Ω are just series elements so they become placeable parts. The physical cross is invisible to the schematic (and that's correct).

2PCB — top-down placement

This is where the swap actually happens. D+ crosses from the top rail to the bottom rail through RJ1, hopping over the straight D− trace. RJ2 sits in-line on D− as the matched partner.

Don't want a trace under a body? If there's room, route D− to skirt around RJ1 instead of passing beneath it — same electrical result, no under-body trace. Near the connector it's usually tighter to just go under, which is fine (next two views show it's safe).

→Build it, step by step

  1. Schematic: drop two 0 Ω onto the D± wires — RJ1 between A6↔B6, RJ2 between A7↔B7. Tap USB2_DP/USB2_DM off each node to the TPD4E05U06 → module.
  2. Assign parts: 0 Ω 0402 footprint + LCSC part on both. Add a note: "RJ1/RJ2 = D± A↔B tie + crossover — see PCB."
  3. Update PCB: Design → Update PCB. RJ1/RJ2 land in the pile.
  4. Place RJ1 at the crossing as the bridge — straddling the D− trace. Place RJ2 in-line on D− right after, as the matched partner.
  5. Route: D+ hops top→bottom through RJ1; D− runs straight beneath RJ1's body (thin, soldermask-covered) then through RJ2.
  6. Match & tighten: equalize the two trace lengths through the zone, keep the pair coupled, and put the crossover close to the connector/ESD.
